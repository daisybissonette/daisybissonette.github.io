High School

high school resources

This page collects resources I wish I'd known about in high school — scholarships, free campus visit programs, summer research opportunities, free learning tools, and competitions. I grew up in Upstate New York, so I've tried to include NY-specific options alongside national programs. This is a living list; if you know of something I've missed, feel free to reach out.

1. Scholarship Resources

Scholarships can come from your state, local community foundations, national foundations, and STEM-specific organizations. Start local — local scholarships often have fewer applicants and are worth applying for even if the award is smaller.

New York State / Local

  • NYS Tuition Assistance Program (TAP) — Need-based grant for NY residents attending NY colleges. Not a scholarship you apply to separately — eligibility is determined through your FAFSA/TAP application.
  • Excelsior Scholarship — Free tuition at SUNY and CUNY schools for NY residents meeting income thresholds. Check the income cap for your family size each year.
  • NYS STEM Incentive Program — Full SUNY tuition for NY residents who graduate in the top 10% of their high school class and pursue a STEM degree, committing to work in NY after graduation.
  • NYS Regents Awards / Military Service Scholarships — Several NYS awards for children of veterans, National Guard members, and those who served in specific conflicts. Check the HESC site for the full list.
  • Community Foundation of Central New York — Offers many local scholarships for students in Central/Upstate NY. Worth checking their scholarship portal each fall.
  • Your county's community foundation or local Rotary club — Nearly every county in NYS has a community foundation or civic organization that offers scholarships. Google "[your county] community foundation scholarship" — these are often low-competition.
  • NYS EOP / HEOP Programs — The Educational Opportunity Program (at SUNY) and Higher Education Opportunity Program (at private colleges) provide financial support and academic services for first-gen and low-income NY students.
  • Your high school guidance counselor's scholarship list — Many local scholarships are only announced through guidance offices. Ask your counselor for a list of everything available to seniors in your school or district.

National

  • QuestBridge National College Match — Matches high-achieving, low-income students with full four-year scholarships at 50+ selective colleges. Apply in fall of senior year. One of the most valuable scholarship programs in the country.
  • Gates Scholarship — Full scholarship for exceptional, Pell-eligible minority students of high academic achievement. Highly competitive; cover all unmet financial need.
  • Coca-Cola Scholars Program — $20,000 merit scholarship for 150 students per year. Emphasis on leadership and community involvement. Apply in fall of senior year.
  • Coca-Cola Community College Award — Separate $1,000 award for students attending two-year community colleges.
  • Elks National Foundation Most Valuable Student Scholarship — Merit and need-based, open to US citizens. Up to $50,000 total award. Apply through your local Elks lodge — worth checking if there's one near you.
  • UNCF Scholarships — United Negro College Fund administers hundreds of scholarships for Black students. Searchable database on their site.
  • Hispanic Scholarship Fund — Scholarships for Hispanic/Latino students at all levels of higher education.
  • Regeneron Science Talent Search — America's oldest and most prestigious science and math competition for high school seniors, with scholarships up to $250,000. See also the Competitions section below.

2. Fly-In / Fly-Out Programs

Fly-in programs are free campus visit experiences for prospective applicants who are first-generation college students, low-income, or from underrepresented groups. The college pays for your travel, housing, and meals. These are a fantastic way to see campuses you might not otherwise be able to visit, and they can substantially strengthen your application. Apply as early as possible — many programs have rolling deadlines in fall of junior or senior year.

  • Princeton FYRE (First Year Retreat and Experience) — Two-day fall fly-in for first-gen and low-income prospective students. Hosted by Princeton's Office of Admission.
  • Harvard Preview Weekend — Multi-day fall fly-in for prospective applicants from underrepresented backgrounds. Includes classes, student panels, and a full campus experience.
  • Yale FSAP (First-Year Scholars at Yale Preview) — Yale's fly-in for first-gen and low-income prospective students.
  • MIT SPARK — MIT's fall fly-in program for students from underrepresented and underserved backgrounds.
  • Colleges That Change Lives (CTCL) — Many CTCL member schools (e.g., Beloit, Allegheny, Wooster) run their own fly-in programs. Check individual school sites.
  • Stanford Diversity Open Campus — Free visit program for prospective students from underrepresented communities.
  • UChicago Metcalf Scholars / Collegiate Scholars — The University of Chicago runs several fly-in and preview programs; check their admissions site under "Visit."
  • Duke Blue Devil Days — Duke's fly-in experience for first-gen and underrepresented prospective students in the fall.
  • Vanderbilt MOSAIC — Vanderbilt's fall fly-in for first-gen, low-income, and underrepresented prospective applicants.
  • Rice Owl Days — Rice University's fly-in program for prospective students from underserved communities.
  • QuestBridge Partner College Visit Days — Many QuestBridge partner colleges host separate fly-in programs for QuestBridge applicants. Check the partner college list.
  • Caltech RISE (Recognizing and Inspiring Student Excellence) — Caltech's fall fly-in experience for underrepresented high school seniors.
  • School-specific fly-in programs — Most highly selective schools have at least one fly-in program. Google "[school name] fly-in program" or "[school name] first-generation visit" to find them.

3. Summer Research & STEM Programs

These programs offer hands-on research experience, rigorous coursework, or both — often for free or with a stipend. Competition is intense for the most selective programs, but there are opportunities at many levels. Apply to a mix of reach and likely programs.

New York State / Regional

  • Simons Summer Research Program (Stony Brook) — Free six-week research program at Stony Brook University for NY high school students, with a stipend. Competitive; work directly with faculty on research projects.
  • Brookhaven National Lab High School Research Program — Seven-week paid summer research program at Brookhaven (Long Island, NY) for high school students in STEM.
  • COSMOS (California-style, but Cornell has one) — Intensive residential STEM program. California runs COSMOS at UC campuses; check if Cornell or other NY institutions run similar programs.
  • Collegiate Scholars Program — If you're in NYC, NYC programs like those through CUNY or Columbia's community outreach are worth researching.
  • Local college and university summer programs — Many NY colleges (Cornell, RPI, Clarkson, etc.) run summer programs for high schoolers. Check admissions and outreach pages at schools near you.

National

  • Research Science Institute (RSI) — Arguably the most prestigious free summer STEM program for high schoolers. Six weeks at MIT; competitive research with scientists. Very competitive.
  • MIT PRIMES — Year-long math and computer science research program for high schoolers, run by MIT. Culminates at a research conference. Also has a reading program (PRIMES Circle).
  • Summer Science Program (SSP) — Five-week residential program in astrophysics (or biochemistry or genomics). Students determine the orbit of an asteroid using real telescope data. Need-blind admissions and generous financial aid.
  • Simons Summer Research (national component) — See above; also note that the Simons Foundation funds other STEM opportunities worth exploring on their website.
  • Garcia Research Scholar Program (Stony Brook) — Seven-week polymer and materials science research program open to students nationwide.
  • NASA Internship Programs — NASA offers various summer internship opportunities; eligibility varies. Check the NASA OSTEM (Office of STEM Engagement) site for high school options.
  • Pathways to Science: HS Program Database — Searchable database of summer research opportunities for underrepresented students in STEM. Excellent starting point.
  • Canada/USA Mathcamp — Intensive five-week summer math program for students 13–18. Need-blind with financial aid available.
  • PROMYS (Program in Mathematics for Young Scientists) — Six-week number theory residential program at Boston University. Generous financial aid; free for families earning under a certain threshold.
  • MIT Minority Introduction to Engineering and Science (MITES) — Free six-week STEM program at MIT for rising seniors from underrepresented groups. Highly selective and highly regarded.

4. Free Textbooks & Learning Resources

You don't need to buy expensive textbooks to learn advanced material. Here are some excellent free resources.

General Learning Platforms

  • Khan Academy — Free courses in math, science, computing, and more, from middle school through college level. Excellent for filling gaps and self-pacing.
  • MIT OpenCourseWare — Free lecture notes, problem sets, and exams from MIT courses across every subject. Great for going beyond what's taught in high school.
  • Coursera — Online courses from universities worldwide. Many courses are free to audit; financial aid is available for certificates.
  • edX — Similar to Coursera; offers courses from MIT, Harvard, and others. Many free to audit.
  • Brilliant.org — Interactive math, science, and CS courses. Some free content; subscription for full access. Good for competition prep and deep understanding.

Free Textbooks

  • OpenStax — Free, peer-reviewed textbooks for college and AP-level courses including Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Calculus, Statistics, and more.
  • Open Textbook Library — University of Minnesota's catalog of free, reviewed open textbooks across many disciplines.
  • Project Gutenberg — Free ebooks of classic literature and older scientific texts in the public domain.

Astronomy-Specific Resources

  • Crash Course Astronomy (YouTube) — 47-episode series covering the entire solar system, stars, galaxies, and cosmology. Free, engaging, and accurate.
  • Astronomy Notes (Nick Strobel) — Free online astronomy textbook covering introductory astronomy at the college level.
  • Sky & Telescope — Excellent resource for observing guides, news in astronomy, and beginner-friendly explanations of recent discoveries.
  • NASA Astronomy Picture of the Day (APOD) — A new astronomy image every day with an explanation written by professional astronomers. Great for curiosity and context.
  • Stellarium (free planetarium software) — Free, open-source planetarium for your desktop or browser. Explore the sky from anywhere on Earth.

5. Science Olympiad, Olympiads & Competitions

Competitions are a great way to learn, meet peers who share your interests, and build a portfolio for college applications. Many have no entry fee.

  • Science Olympiad — Team-based competition with 23 events spanning biology, chemistry, physics, earth science, engineering, and more. Competitions run at invitational, regional, state, and national levels.
  • US Astronomy and Astrophysics Olympiad (USAAAO) — National astronomy competition; top performers represent the US at the International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics (IOAA).
  • International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics (IOAA) — Annual international competition for high school students in astronomy and astrophysics.
  • DOE National Science Bowl — Fast-paced question-and-answer competition in science and math, run by the US Department of Energy. Regional competitions lead to a national tournament in Washington, DC.
  • Regeneron Science Talent Search (STS) — The nation's oldest and most prestigious pre-college science competition. Submit an original research project as a senior; top 40 finalists compete in Washington, DC for awards up to $250,000.
  • Regeneron ISEF (International Science and Engineering Fair) — The world's largest international pre-college science competition. Qualify through local and state fairs.
  • AMC / AIME / USAMO (Math competitions) — The Mathematical Association of America runs a ladder of math competitions: AMC 8/10/12 → AIME → USAMO. Top scorers qualify for the US Math Olympiad team.
  • F=ma / US Physics Olympiad (USAPhO) — The American Association of Physics Teachers runs a competition leading to the US Physics Olympiad team and the IPhO (International Physics Olympiad).
  • USA Biology Olympiad (USABO) — National biology competition with semifinals and finals; top students represent the US at the International Biology Olympiad.
  • US National Chemistry Olympiad (USNCO) — National chemistry competition run by the American Chemical Society, leading to the International Chemistry Olympiad.

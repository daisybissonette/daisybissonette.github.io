<p>
  Resources for applying to and paying for college. I grew up in Upstate New York, so NY-specific options are included alongside national ones.
</p>

<p>
  Deadlines and eligibility change yearly. If something here is out of date, <a href="/contact/">let me know</a>.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>1. Application Fee Waivers</h2>

<ul>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.commonapp.org/apply/fee-waivers" target="_blank">Common App Fee Waiver</a></strong> —
    Requested inside the Common App. No limit on the number of schools it covers. Your school counselor can confirm eligibility.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/registration/fee-waivers" target="_blank">SAT Fee Waiver</a></strong> /
    <strong><a href="https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act/registration/fee-waivers.html" target="_blank">ACT Fee Waiver</a></strong> —
    Cover the test and score sends, and getting these waivers usually makes you automatically eligible for college application fee waivers.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Email the admissions office</strong> —
    If you don't formally qualify but the fee is a hardship, ask! 
  </li>
</ul>

<p>Common eligibility criteria:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Enrolled in or eligible for Free/Reduced Price Lunch</li>
  <li>Family income within USDA Income Eligibility Guidelines</li>
  <li>Enrolled in a program serving low-income students (TRIO, Upward Bound, GEAR UP)</li>
  <li>Living in federally subsidized public housing or a foster home, or experiencing homelessness</li>
  <li>Ward of the state or orphan</li>
</ul>

<p>Some colleges also waive the fee if you visit campus or attend an info session. Check each school's admissions FAQ.</p>

<h2>2. Scholarships</h2>

<p>Scholarships come from your state, local community foundations, national foundations, and STEM organizations. </p>

    Hub for New York State aid: the <strong>Tuition Assistance Program (TAP)</strong>, the <strong>Excelsior Scholarship</strong> (SUNY/CUNY tuition under an income cap), and the <strong>NYS STEM Incentive Program</strong> (full SUNY tuition for top-10% students in a STEM degree, with a commitment to work in NY afterward).
  </li>
  <!-- <li>

  <li>
    <strong>County community foundations</strong> —
    Most NY counties have one administering local scholarships. For far north upstate NY (e.g. Jefferson, Lewis, and St. Lawrence County), I got a great scholarship from the <strong><a href="https://nnycf.org/scholarships/scholarship-overview/" target="_blank">Northern New York Community Foundation</a></strong>.

  <li>

<h3>National</h3>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.thegatesscholarship.org/" target="_blank">The Gates Scholarship</a></strong> —
    Covers full cost of attendance not met by other aid, for Pell-eligible students from minority backgrounds. Opens in July, closes mid-September of senior year.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.coca-colascholarsfoundation.org/" target="_blank">Coca-Cola Scholars Program</a></strong> —
    $20,000 for 150 students per year, weighted toward leadership and community involvement. Opens in August. A separate award exists for community college students.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.elks.org/scholars/" target="_blank">Elks Most Valuable Student Scholarship</a></strong> —
    Merit and need-based, up to $50,000 total. Applied for through your local Elks lodge.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.jkcf.org/our-scholarships/college-scholarship-program/" target="_blank">Jack Kent Cooke Foundation College Scholarship</a></strong> —
    Up to $55,000 per year for high-achieving students with financial need.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://scholarships.uncf.org/" target="_blank">UNCF Scholarships</a></strong> —
    Hundreds of scholarships for Black students through one searchable portal and a single profile.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.hsf.net/" target="_blank">Hispanic Scholarship Fund</a></strong> —
    Scholarships for Hispanic/Latino students at all levels, plus career services and mentoring.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.aps.org/funding-recognition/scholarship" target="_blank">APS Scholarships</a></strong> —
    American Physical Society scholarships for students entering physics.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://swe.org/scholarships/" target="_blank">Society of Women Engineers Scholarships</a></strong> —
    One application covers a large pool of awards in engineering and related fields.
  </li>
</ul>

<p>Any scholarship charging an application fee is a scam!</p>

<h2>3. Fly-In &amp; Campus Visit Programs</h2>

<p>
  Fly-ins are free campus visits for prospective applicants, typically first-generation, low-income, or from underrepresented backgrounds. The college covers travel, housing, and meals.
</p>

<p>
  Many of these programs were restructured after the 2023 Supreme Court decision on race-conscious admissions, with criteria shifting toward first-generation and limited-income status. Some were renamed or discontinued, so the lists below are more reliable than individual program links.
</p>

<h3>Annually updated lists</h3>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.collegetransitions.com/blog/colleges-with-fly-in-programs/" target="_blank">College Transitions — Colleges With Fly-In Programs</a></strong> —
    Updated each admissions cycle, with links to each application.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://blog.collegevine.com/college-fly-in-and-diversity-programs-a-complete-list" target="_blank">CollegeVine — Fly-In and Diversity Programs</a></strong> —
    Aggregated list with eligibility notes.
  </li>
</ul>

<h2>4. Free Learning Resources</h2>

<h3>Courses &amp; platforms</h3>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.khanacademy.org/" target="_blank">Khan Academy</a></strong> —
    Math, science, and computing from middle school through early college. Also hosts free official SAT practice. I used this for all my test prep!
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://ocw.mit.edu/" target="_blank">MIT OpenCourseWare</a></strong> —
    Lecture notes, problem sets, exams, and video from MIT courses. The "Scholar" versions are built for self-study.
  </li>
</ul>

<h3>Free textbooks</h3>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://openstax.org/" target="_blank">OpenStax</a></strong> —
    Peer-reviewed textbooks for college and AP-level Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Calculus, Statistics, and <a href="https://openstax.org/details/books/astronomy-2e" target="_blank">Astronomy</a>. Published by Rice University.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/" target="_blank">Open Textbook Library</a></strong> —
    University of Minnesota's catalog of reviewed open textbooks.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://libretexts.org/" target="_blank">LibreTexts</a></strong> —
    Free textbook-level material in physics, chemistry, and math, organized by course level.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.gutenberg.org/" target="_blank">Project Gutenberg</a></strong> —
    Public-domain ebooks, including historical scientific texts.
  </li>
</ul>

<h3>Coding</h3>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.freecodecamp.org/" target="_blank">freeCodeCamp</a></strong> —
    Project-based curricula in web development, Python, and data analysis.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://www.py4e.com/" target="_blank">Python for Everybody</a></strong> —
    Introduction to Python assuming no prior programming.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong><a href="https://colab.research.google.com/" target="_blank">Google Colab</a></strong> —
    Browser-based Python notebooks with no local installation.
  </li>
</ul>
